Pipeline watch

Helene permanent work · North Carolina

$890 million in Davis-Bacon-triggering work.

FEMA has now obligated $2.40 billion in public assistance across North Carolina for Hurricane Helene, spread over 348 applicants.

Roughly $890 million of that sits in permanent work categories — roads and bridges, water control facilities, buildings and equipment, utilities, parks and recreation. Those are the categories that trigger Davis-Bacon.

Here’s what makes this pipeline different from a state DOT letting.

FEMA money flows to local governments as subrecipients — counties, towns, water districts, school systems. Those entities procure the construction. Many have never administered a Davis-Bacon project in their history. They’ll be writing the contracts, reviewing certified payrolls, and holding the compliance obligation.

That obligation flows straight down to you.

What to do

Don’t wait for an RFP to appear on a portal. Call the county or municipal engineering office directly. Ask three questions: when does construction procurement open, who is the engineer of record, and are you accepting contractor information for the bidders list. Fifteen minutes. Most contractors never make the call.

The practitioner note

How to tell whether Davis-Bacon applies before you bid

This month’s NCDOT letting made the case better than I could.

Five proposals let on July 21. Two of them were bridge preservation. One was federal-aid. One was state funded. Nothing in the letting list, the county, the scope description, or the dollar value distinguished them.

Get this wrong in one direction and you’ve priced compliance you don’t owe, and lost the bid. Get it wrong in the other and you’ve committed to certified payroll, wage determinations, and worker interviews you never budgeted.

Here’s how to know, for each of the three funding types:

Federal-aid — state DOT administering federal highway money. Look for a federal project number in the proposal: formats like STP-5115(209), NHPP-5118(346), or BRDG-030-900. STP is Surface Transportation Program. NHPP is National Highway Performance Program. If a federal project number is attached, Davis-Bacon applies and a wage determination will be in the proposal documents. For NCDOT, the Item C award letter states funding source explicitly. If there’s no federal project number and the proposal says STATE FUNDED, Davis-Bacon doesn’t apply — though state prevailing wage law may.

Federal direct — contracting with the agency itself. Every federal construction contract over $2,000 is covered. No exceptions for size, agency, or scope. The wage determination is in the solicitation on SAM.gov. Your certified payrolls go to the contracting officer, not a state DOT.

Federally assisted — grant money through a non-federal entity. This is the one that catches people. FEMA public assistance, EPA State Revolving Fund, CDBG, FTA transit grants, FAA airport improvement money. The check comes from a county or a town, so it feels local — but the underlying federal funding carries Davis-Bacon with it. Ask the awarding entity directly, in writing, whether the project is federally funded and whether a wage determination applies. Get the answer before you submit a number.

What to do

On the next opportunity you look at, find the funding source before you find the quantities. Two minutes, and it determines your entire administrative cost structure.
